Tournament Ops

Federation licensing, logistics, and matchday delivery.

From kickoff to broadcast — we handle the parts of running a tournament that most organizations underestimate until it is too late to fix them.

What we actually do

Running a tournament well is mostly invisible work. Nobody notices good logistics — they only notice bad ones. We handle federation licensing and approvals, venue booking and setup, official scheduling, medical and safety planning, and the dozens of smaller coordination points that decide whether a tournament day runs smoothly or falls apart by the second match.

Our work with the Dubai Open for Football Academies — run in partnership with the Dubai FA and FIFA's Talent Development Scheme — is a good example of what full-scale tournament operations looks like end to end: multiple academies, age-group brackets, official ceremonies, sponsor activations, and full-day matchday delivery.

Why it's run this way

Tournaments involve a lot of stakeholders who each care about a different piece — the federation cares about licensing and standards, academies care about fair scheduling, sponsors care about visibility, players and parents just want the day to run on time. Someone has to hold all of that together, and that's the actual job.

We treat matchday delivery the way live event production treats a broadcast: a fixed schedule, clear ownership of every moving part, and contingency plans for the things that inevitably go sideways — weather, delayed officials, a venue change at the last minute.

What this looks like in practice

  • Federation and governing-body licensing and compliance
  • Bracket structuring, fixture scheduling, and official assignment
  • Venue logistics — setup, signage, medical cover, crowd flow
  • Sponsor and partner activation on tournament day
  • Closing ceremonies and results management
